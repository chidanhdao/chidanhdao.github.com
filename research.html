Rewards, Credit Access, and Inequality in the Credit Card Market

Job Market Paper, 2026

Abstract: his paper examines the distributional consequences of credit cards by accounting for their dual role as payment instruments and sources of unsecured credit. Existing studies typically frame these concerns around rebates earned net of interest charges and fee, which fails to capture the welfare gains from credit access. Using microdata from the Bank of Canada’s Digital Wallet and Payment Trends Survey, I estimate a structural model of consumer payment choice and show that ignoring the value of credit access overstates the Gini coefficient of welfare gains from credit cards by 46%. Moreover, the Gini co- efficient rises by 6% in a counterfactual that requires cardholders to repay their balances in full. This result implies that access to revolv- ing credit has progressive distributional effects, and contradicts the perception of credit card debt as exploitative.
